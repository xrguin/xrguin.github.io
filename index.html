01 / UNDERWATER ROBOTICSUniversity of Notre Dame

AUV Dynamical
Modeling and Control

I build learning-based models and controllers that help underwater robots follow planned paths. This project brings together simulation, online model updates, and camera-based tracking, with testing in a real pool.

Dynamics modelingAdaptive controlComputer vision
01

Data-driven modeling
in simulation

Train models to predict an underwater robot’s motion using simulation data, then evaluate their predictions against measurements from pool experiments.

02

Online model update

Update how the model responds to control commands as new measurements arrive, helping address differences between simulation and the real robot.

03

Visual localization

Track the robot’s position and heading using an overhead camera and visual markers, providing feedback for navigation tests.

Explore the models and pool experiments
02 / ROBOT NAVIGATIONUniversity of Notre Dame · UR 2025

Dynamic Obstacle
Avoidance

I developed a method that helps a robot anticipate obstacles outside its view by learning from a nearby robot’s movement. Simulation tests combine these predictions with a navigation controller to reduce collisions.

Sequence learningMotion predictionRobot navigation

03 / POWER SYSTEMSResearch collaboration with ORNL

Probability Density Function Control for Power Grid Systems

I use machine learning and optimization to control how power-grid signals fluctuate. This work focuses on shaping the full probability distribution of frequency and voltage fluctuations, in collaboration with Oak Ridge National Laboratory.

Machine learningPredictive controlMATLAB / Simulink

Summer 2024 · ORNL internship

Modeling & optimization

Built a neural-network model of frequency fluctuations, added environmental disturbances to a grid simulation, and developed an optimization-based controller.

Subsequent collaboration · through March 2026

Learned predictive control

Combined neural networks with predictive control to steer voltage fluctuations toward a desired distribution in power-system simulations.

Explore the controller and simulation results
04 / SOFT ROBOTICSUniversity of California San Diego

Scalable tactile sensing
for soft skins

I helped develop a flexible skin that identifies where it is touched. The design uses six pressure sensors to monitor a nine-cell array, with demonstrations of touches at multiple locations and contact detection on a robot arm.

Background

About me

I work at the intersection of learning, control, and physical systems.

I am a graduate research assistant in the Robotics and Autonomy Research Lab at Notre Dame, advised by Dr. Mengxue Hou. Previously, I worked on soft sensing with Dr. Michael Tolley at UC San Diego and hydrokinetic-turbine modeling with Dr. Clarissa Belloni at Ohio State.

My experience also includes a summer 2024 research internship at Oak Ridge National Laboratory and mechanical-engineering internships at Trina Solar and SAIC Volkswagen.
